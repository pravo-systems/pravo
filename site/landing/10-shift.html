Kept, revisited, tried

The providers stay. The foundation is new.

OpenTofu and Terraform got the providers and the plan-and-apply loop right. Where ten years of operating them point elsewhere, pravo starts from a clean slate: the state, the plan, the policy and the language.

Kept

What worked

  • Every OpenTofu and Terraform provider, unchanged.
  • Declarative desired state, not a program that runs.
  • A plan you read before anything changes.
Revisited

What ten years of operating it taught

  • State is one record per resource, not one file.
  • A plan reaches what a change can affect and skips the rest.
  • Locks are per resource, so applies on different resources run at once.
  • A refresh age says when to read real state again.
  • Policy as code is built in and uses the same language.
Tried

A different footing

  • CUE, a constraint language, in place of markup with imperative extras.
  • Modules need no variable or output wiring, and any field can be overridden.
  • Validation is just the schema, checked before any provider starts.

# web/dev

Development material for the interface. The Go server does not serve this directory, and the page loads nothing from it unless its URL says `mock=`.

## Run the interface without the server

```sh
cd internal/serve/web
python3 -m http.server 8830 --bind 127.0.0.1
```

Open `http://127.0.0.1:8830/index.html?mock=platform`. The text between `?` and `#` selects what answers `/api/*`; the fragment is the view, as in the real page.

| Query | Effect |
|---|---|
| `mock=platform` | About 45 resources and eight outputs: namespaces `network`, `api` and `edge` with nested namespaces, a plan with creates, updates, one replace and one delete, a drifted and a gone node, a spec-only node, an orphan, a locked node, sensitive attributes. The outputs are a plain one, a sensitive one, one that is not recorded yet (`cache_endpoint`), one that reads two other outputs (`summary`), an orphan (`legacy_url`) and two that sit in namespaces. |
| `mock=fleet` | About 170 nodes: regions, services and resources three namespaces deep, with for-each groups of twelve or more siblings of one type, so that clustering shows. |
| `mock=big` | About 1900 nodes in 30 namespaces, flat groups of siblings, and 27 outputs at the root, which the page groups into one card. The first view shows about twenty items. |
| `mock=estate` | 141 nodes in ten namespaces: about forty services, data stores, an edge, a network, secrets, feeds and a bucket with two outputs, with environment variables, built-in values, ephemeral resources and data sources at the root and five outputs. It stands for a real estate of a hundred resources and more, and the graph of it is the test of the layout. |
| `mock=empty` | No nodes: the empty state. |
| `mock=specerror` | `spec_error` set, state only. |
| `plan=plan-2f9c1a4e` | The server lays that stored plan over the graph itself, as `pravo serve --plan plan-2f9c1a4e` does: it is the plan of the graph and the page opens with it selected. Taking it off in the page is `plan=none` in the fragment. |
| `access=NAME` | The Access view answers `access.NAME.json`. Without it the platform is sealed to ada, dan and eve and ada is the reader (`platform`); other mocks are not sealed (`off`). The others: `immutable`, `drift` (the file and the store differ, and a store reader has a name that is not usable), `locked` (no identity: the graph lists every record as sealed and the outputs show `sealed`), `notreader` (an identity that is not a reader, the same view), `missing` (no readers file), `migrating`, `invalid` (the readers file does not load) and `solo` (sealed to one reader: the warning), `downgraded` (the repository lists readers and the state is not sealed: the verdict `expected-sealed` and the suggestion `check-downgrade`). Open it at `#/?view=access`. |
| `auth=401` | Every `/api/` request answers 401: the page asks for the address with the token. |
| `fail=detail` | `/api/detail` answers 500 with a message: the error toast. |
| `latency=300` | Delays every answer by that many milliseconds: the loading skeletons. |

Only the platform has detail and source answers written out (`detail.db.json`, `detail.password.json`, `detail.drifted.json`, `source.db.json`, `source.password.json`: `api.db`, `api.db_password` and `edge.lb`; `detail.output.json`, `detail.outputmap.json`, `detail.outputsecret.json` and `source.output.json`: the outputs `url`, `summary` and `db_password`). For every other node the shim makes the answer from the graph, from a small table of attributes by type: schema known or not (`aws_wafv2_web_acl` has no schema), sensitive attributes, a history from the revision, the plan's change, a lock.

The platform also has seven plans stored in the state: `plans.platform.json` is the answer of `/api/plans`, and `plan.2f9c1a4e.json` and its six siblings are the answers of `/api/plan?id=plan-2f9c1a4e` and so on. Four of the plans can be applied: `ana@acme` and `ben@acme` both change `api.db`, which has the ring of an overlap and two author badges, `dee@acme` has applied one of its two changes (`partial`: the applied one is marked `applied` in its touches and is on no node, and the overlaps of a plan count only the changes that are left), and `kim@acme` has a plan that is `partly-stale` (`plan-b86e2d14`): `api.role` moved in the state, so it conflicts, and `api.policy` has to be applied with it; `api.secret` and `api.logs` are the part that can be applied, by `--only`. The plan has `conflicts`, `unapplied`, `apply_only` and `apply_live`, a `conflict` mark on two of its touches, and its badge on all four nodes; its conflicting changes do not count for an overlap. One plan is stale, one applied and one expired. A listing row (`plans.platform.json`) has no `reason`, `command` or `git_revision`; the overlay (`plan.*.json`) has them, as the server sends them. An overlap is `{id, count, addrs}` and is symmetric. `detail` and `source` take `plan=ID` (or `plan=none`) and the shim makes the answer for that plan from the overlay. The other mocks have no stored plans: the section of the rail is not there.

The page sets `data-ready="1"` on `<html>` when the first view is drawn, which a screenshot program can wait for.

In a mock session `window.__mock` has `setAccess(name)` (swaps the Access document for another fixture and sends the event that says `access`), `mutate()` (changes three nodes and sends a `change` event), `storePlan()` (stores a seventh plan, `plan-d4b7e913` by `dee@acme`, which also changes `api.db`, and sends the event that says `plans`), `collectPlan(id)` (removes a stored plan, with its event), `drop()` and `restore()` (the connection dot), and `window.__errors` collects the errors and warnings of the page. `window.__pravo` exposes the state, the graph view and a few actions, for a headless run; the page defines it only when the URL has `mock=` or `debug=`.

## The fragment

The view is in the fragment: `#/?sel=ADDR&tab=state|source|schema|history|change&collapse=A,B&expand=C,D&theme=light|dark&q=TEXT&filter=plan|drifted|sensitive|orphans|declared|outputs|env&plan=plan-2f9c1a4e&view=graph|table&motion=off&zoom=fit`. Values are URL-encoded; lists are comma separated, each item encoded. Besides those:

| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `sel` | A node address, or the path of a namespace. A node that a collapsed namespace hides is revealed unless `collapse` is given. |
| `plan` | A plan stored in the state, by its whole id, laid over the graph: its actions replace those of the nodes, the Change tab and the source answer for it, and the first view is made for it (the namespaces it touches open first). `plan=none` takes off the plan that the server laid itself (`pravo serve --plan ID`). Without it the page shows the plan of the server, if any. An id that the state does not hold is told in a toast and ignored. The page writes it when a plan is selected, and not when the selected plan is the one the server laid. |
| `collapse`, `expand` | Namespace paths, applied on top of the first view (see below). `all` means every namespace. `expand` also takes cluster ids (`c:NAMESPACE\|MODE\|TYPE`). |
| `zoom` | `fit` (the default) or a scale, centred on the selection when there is one. |
| `motion=off` | No animation or transition at all (reduced motion has the same effect). Use it for screenshots. |
| `palette=1`, `pq=TEXT` | Open the search palette, with a query. |
| `help=1` | Open the sheet of shortcuts. |

How the page treats the stored plans when the project moves: the graph and the list of plans are read together and read again (twice at most) until they are of one revision, so a change that lands between the two requests does not leave a list that is newer than the graph; a refresh that was begun before the person chose a plan does not override that choice (`planMark` in `app.js`). An overlay that cannot be read (`/api/plan` fails, or the plan was collected meanwhile) is told in a toast and the page goes on with the graph and the list: it is not fatal, and a pasted `plan=` that the state does not hold is told the same way. The warnings of the plan that the server laid itself (`--plan ID`) are shown while that plan is selected and go when another plan, or none, is. The button that copies `pravo apply ID` turns to a cross and announces "Could not copy" when the clipboard refuses it. The initials of an author are taken by whole characters (a surrogate pair is not cut in two). A refresh that redraws the section of the rail keeps the focus on the row, filter or control that had it.

## The first view

When the page loads it chooses which namespaces are open, with `firstView` in `assets/model.js`: breadth first, one level of namespaces at a time. Everything starts as a card; a namespace opens when the picture that follows still fits the canvas at a zoom of at least 0.8 (a 14 px name is 11 px on screen) and within a budget of items that grows with the area of the canvas (about 75 at 1440 by 900). Namespaces that the plan, the drift, a spec-only or a state-only resource touch go first, then the larger ones; namespaces of one shape (three regions) open together or not at all. The first view does not depend on anything that a person chooses (the selection, a filter, a search): the fragment writes the open and closed namespaces relative to it, so a reload, which works it out again, must reach the same answer. A selection opens the namespaces above it afterwards (`revealSel` in `app.js`), unless a `collapse=` names one of them. `collapse=` and `expand=` are applied on top, so a link means the same thing on any screen as far as the namespaces it names go. A namespace inside a closed one is not written to the fragment (a link cannot say it: `expand=` opens what is above). The key `0` and the toolbar button Reset view go back to the first view (namespaces, groups and camera), keeping the filter and the search. `firstView` is a pure function (no DOM) that takes the canvas and returns the closed namespaces; `node check-firstview.mjs` runs it on the fixtures at several canvas sizes and checks the budget, the zoom, the peers, the forced namespaces and the speed.

## Fixtures

`fixtures/*.json` are answers of the contract in `../../api.go`, field names included and nothing extra. They are made by two programs; run them again after a change:

```sh
node gen-fixtures.js     # platform, fleet, empty, specerror, the platform's detail and source answers, and its stored plans
node gen-big.js          # graph.big.json
node check-fixtures.js   # reads the Go types of api.go and checks every fixture against them
```

`check-fixtures.js` is the contract test: it fails on a key that `api.go` does not have, on a key that is not `omitempty` and is missing, and on a value of the wrong JSON type, and it checks that every edge, namespace and parent exists. It also checks that the stored plans and the graph say the same: every address a plan touches is a node, the plans on a node are the open plans that change it, a node that two of them can apply a change on has `overlap` (a change that a partly stale plan cannot apply does not count), a partly stale plan has conflicts, a part to apply and the command for it, and the other plans have none, the counts of a plan are those of its touches, and each plan has an overlay whose nodes are its touches. The fixtures are named `graph.*`, `detail.*`, `source.*`, `plans.*` (an answer of `/api/plans`, which goes with the `graph.*` of the same name), `plan.*` (an answer of `/api/plan`) and `access.*` (an answer of `/api/access`). The access fixtures are not made by the programs above: they are what the server sends, and `internal/serve/access_fixtures_test.go` writes them (`PRAVO_UPDATE_FIXTURES=1 go test ./internal/serve -run AccessFixtures`) and compares them otherwise. `check-fixtures.js` checks that the state and the verdict are known, that the readers agree with the lists of added and removed readers, that a suggestion has steps of the four kinds, that a command is one line of `pravo` or `git`, and that `access.NAME.json` has the revision of `graph.NAME.json`. The Go side reads the same files in `contract_test.go`.

`node check-keyboard.mjs` drives the Access view in headless Chrome with real key events (`Input.dispatchKeyEvent`, which is what makes `:focus-visible` apply; a programmatic `focus()` does not), through `cdp-driver.mjs`, which serves this directory on a free port and speaks the DevTools protocol over Node's own WebSocket. For every Access fixture, in both themes, at 1250 px and at 375 px, it presses Tab until the focus leaves the page or comes round, then Shift+Tab as many times, and checks that every interactive element of the view is a stop in the order of the page, that Shift+Tab visits the same stops, that each stop matches `:focus-visible`, has a ring of at least 3:1 against what is behind it that no scroll container clips, and has an accessible name (a copy button says what it copies, a suggestion says whether it is open), that Enter and Space open and close a suggestion, that a copy is announced, that Esc hides a tip and leaves the focus, that the skip link moves the focus into the view, and that the accessibility tree (`Accessibility.getFullAXTree`) holds the verdict and the state of You as text, the readers table with its header cells, the marks with names and the headings in order. It takes about a minute; `--quick` runs the platform and drift fixtures only, and `--order drift 1250 light` prints the focus order of one fixture. It needs Chrome (`CHROME` names it) and skips itself when there is none.

`node check-sealed-counts.mjs` checks that a locked state shows a dash, with the title `sealed`, where a count of drifted or sensitive resources would be a zero that only means the records were not opened (the filter chips and the tiles of the project card).

`node check-dquery.mjs` checks the filter box of the Discovery section: the same language over the objects that discoveries found, which cards a term leaves, and what the menu offers.

`node check-query.mjs` checks the filter box: the words and the `column:value` terms, the comma, the minus, the ranges, and what the menu under the box offers.

`node check-layout.mjs [platform fleet estate big]` runs `layout` (`assets/layout.js`, which hands the work to `assets/flow.js`) on the fixtures, closed, open and as the first view, and checks the picture without a browser: no two items overlap, every lane holds its items and stands apart from the lanes that are not its own, and every wire leaves the right edge of what it reads, arrives at the left edge of what reads it, is made of right-angled runs, runs to the right all the way unless its edge closes a cycle, and stays out of every item and every lane that is not its own. It checks as well that an environment variable stands in the first column of its lane and an output in the last. It checks the counts that merged edges carry as well (`badges` in `assets/layout.js` says where each one stands): each sits on its own wire, no two come within five pixels of each other, and none sits on an item that its wire could have avoided. The pictures are laid out for three canvases, 1440 by 900, 1440 by 762 (the canvas of the page at 1440 by 900) and 1920 by 940. It also prints the size of each picture and the zoom that fits it in a 1440 by 900 canvas, which is the number to watch when the layout changes: a picture that needs a zoom under 0.3 is not read, it is looked at.
